## 03 · SEGMENT 1: Incursions   (save as `03_segment1`)

Okay. Incursions. This is the engine of the whole story, so let's get it right.

In Hickman's comics, the multiverse starts dying. Two universes start crashing into each other.

And the point where they collide? It's always Earth.

Picture it. You look up, and there's another Earth in the sky. Getting closer. And it's not a vision. It's real.

The first person to see one is Black Panther. In Wakanda. He watches another Earth fill the sky, and he watches heroes from that world show up trying to save it.

When the two Earths touch, both universes die. Everything. Every planet, every person, gone.

And here's the brutal part. There's only one way to stop it. You destroy the other Earth before they touch.

Your world lives. Theirs doesn't. Billions of people, gone, so yours can keep going.

Now if that sounds familiar, it should. Marvel already planted this. In Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness, we literally heard the word.

That movie even gave us an Illuminati. And who was sitting in that room? Patrick Stewart's Professor X.

Then Strange caused an incursion himself. Clea showed up in the post-credit scene and told him so.

That wasn't a throwaway line. That was a setup.

And this is where it gets dark. Because in the comics, somebody has to decide which Earth dies.

And the people making that call? They're heroes.

## 05 · SEGMENT 3: Time Runs Out   (save as `05_segment3`)

This is where Hickman flips the whole thing upside down. Time Runs Out picks up eight months later. And the Avengers are at war with each other.

Cap got his memories back. And he's furious. He's hunting the Illuminati, and especially Tony.

Meanwhile, the multiverse keeps collapsing. And finally, they find out why. It's the Beyonders.

These are beings so powerful they're above the cosmic gods. They're killing the multiverse on purpose.

They even take out the Living Tribunal. That's how big we're talking.

So who steps up? Not Cap. Not Reed. Doctor Doom.

Doom teams up with Molecule Man, a guy with reality-bending power. And Doom uses that power to do the unthinkable. He kills the Beyonders.

Read that again. Doctor Doom kills the beings who were killing God. And he takes their power for himself.

But it's still too late. The last two Earths collide. The main Marvel universe and the Ultimate universe. Everything ends.

Everything, except what Doom saves. And what he builds next is where Doomsday is clearly heading.

## 06 · SEGMENT 4: God Emperor Doom   (save as `06_segment4`)

This is Secret Wars. Nine issues, 2015. And it's Doom's world now.

Out of the scraps of dead universes, Doom builds one planet. Battleworld. A patchwork of realities stitched together. Different eras, different heroes, all on one map.

There's a zone full of Marvel Zombies, walled off. There's a wasteland where Old Man Logan lives. Every corner is some reality that used to be its own universe.

And at the center? His capital city. Doomstadt. Remember that throne room in the trailer? A lot of fans think that's exactly where we are.

Doom rules it all as God Emperor. He's got Doctor Strange as his right hand, keeping order. He's got an army of Thors as his police.

And here's the part that's genuinely chilling. Doom has Sue Storm as his wife.

He's raising Franklin and Valeria as his own kids. He took Reed's family.

And nobody remembers the old world. To them, this is just how it's always been. They're living lives Doom handed them.

Now think about that trailer line. "All of you have lived stolen lives." Doom pulling Sentinels out of the ground, from a universe that isn't his. That looks a lot like a guy collecting pieces of dead worlds.

And look at who's in this movie. MCU heroes. Fox's X-Men. A Fantastic Four from their own universe. That's a lot of different worlds ending up in one place.

That's a theory, to be clear. Marvel hasn't confirmed any of it. But the pieces line up way too well.

So if Doom is a god, how do you beat a god?

In the comics, the answer isn't a punch. It's a question.
